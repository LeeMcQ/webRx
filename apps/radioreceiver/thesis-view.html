Field & sessions

Exports include bearing (°T/°M), σ, beamwidth, contrast, front/back, level and units, gain/AGC, GPS accuracy and heading source for every fix. GPX adds the RSSI track.

Transmitter estimate

Save fixes from at least two places to locate the transmitter.

Weighted least squares in a local east/north plane: each bearing counts by 1/(range·σ)², so sharp, high-contrast bearings matter more. The orange ellipse holds the transmitter with 95 % confidence if the bearing errors are as estimated; with three fixes the red triangle is the classic cocked hat.

Target & heading

Bearings need a directional antenna (Yagi/LPDA: peak; loop or body-shield: null). The bearing is the centre of the −3 dB lobe measured in linear power, so sweep slowly past the peak both ways. The phone compass reads magnetic north; Auto adds the WMM2025 declination at your GPS position (around −20° in Gauteng, −25° in the Western Cape). Use Mount offset if the phone is not aligned with the boom. Keep gain fixed (no AGC). Each fix is drawn on the map as a line with a ±σ cone.

Browsers can't read Wi-Fi RSSI or cellular RSRP, so no dBm is shown unless the Radio Bridge app supplies one. Link quality is scored mainly from round-trip time. Chrome caps downlink at 10 Mbps (rounded to 25 kbps), rounds RTT to 25 ms, and often omits connection type on desktop.

What a browser can and can't see

  • Can: connection type, effective type (4g/3g…), estimated downlink and round-trip time, data saver (Network Information API, Chrome/Edge). Shown above and logged when the source is Wi-Fi or Cellular.
  • Can't: scan Wi-Fi networks, read RSSI/SSID/BSSID, or read cell ID, band or signal (RSRP/RSRQ). No browser exposes these.
  • For real RF: use an SDR in SA — tap a band below to tune it. For phone radio readings, the Radio Bridge Android app (branch radio-bridge, in progress) is planned to feed Wi-Fi, cellular, BLE and GNSS data into RM.

    Pre-drive calibration

    Measure the receiver's own noise and the vehicle's noise at the settings you'll drive with, then set the threshold a guard band above the noise floor (thesis §3.4 and §4.3.1). Pins below the threshold are kept but flagged, and the map hides them by default.

    1 · Check the setup

      2 · Receiver noise — 50 Ω dummy load §3.4.4.2

      1. Remove the antenna and fit the 50 Ω dummy load to the antenna port.
      2. If you can, move the receiver away from the laptop or phone on a USB extension cable.

      3 · Vehicle noise — antenna on, engine running §3.4.4.3 · §4.3.1

      1. Refit the antenna exactly as it will be for the drive (roof mount, ferrite choke, short coax).
      2. Start the engine and switch on the air-con, lights and radio.
      3. Run the laptop from its battery — not an inverter or car charger.
